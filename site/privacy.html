Privacy Policy

Last updated: 21 November 2026

xrncal is a calendar application for Windows and Linux. It runs entirely on your computer. The developer operates no servers, receives no data from the app, and has no way to see your calendars or your account.

What the app handles

  • Calendar data — the calendars, events, attendees and reminders belonging to the accounts you connect, so that the app can display and edit them.
  • Basic account identity — the email address and display name of each connected account, used to label that account in the interface.
  • Credentials — the OAuth tokens (or, for CalDAV, the username and password) needed to reach your provider, and the OAuth client ID and secret if you enter your own in the app.
  • Settings — your preferences, and the time zones this computer has been set to, so that an event opens in the zone you were in on its date.

Where it is kept

All of it is stored locally, in a SQLite database inside the application's own profile directory on your computer. In the same directory the app keeps its automatic backups of that database (one a week, the last four, unless you turn them off, and a copy taken before you restore one) and a small log file of startup, sync failures, backups and update errors, which never holds event text or passwords. Nothing is uploaded anywhere by the app.

Credentials are encrypted before they are written, with Electron's safeStorage, and only the encrypted form is kept in the database. The key stays with the operating system: on Windows, its data protection API (DPAPI), tied to your Windows account; on Linux, the system keyring — GNOME Keyring, KeePassXC or another Secret Service, or KDE Wallet. A copy of the database alone therefore does not reveal them. If a Linux system has no keyring available, the app refuses to store the credential rather than fall back to plain text or to the fixed key built into Chromium.

What leaves your computer

The app makes network requests to these destinations only:

  • Google's OAuth and Calendar API endpoints, if you connect a Google account.
  • Microsoft's identity and Microsoft Graph endpoints, if you connect a Microsoft account.
  • The CalDAV server whose address you enter, if you connect a CalDAV or iCloud account.
  • The address of a calendar you subscribe to by link, to refresh it.
  • GitHub, to check for a new version of the app and download it. The requests carry no calendar data and nothing about you, and Check for updates in Settings turns them off.

There are no other outbound requests. The app contains no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no advertising and no third-party trackers; its fonts are part of the app rather than loaded from the web. Holiday calendars are generated on your computer rather than fetched from anywhere.

Google user data

xrncal's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

The app requests these scopes, and only these:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar — to read your calendars and events and to save the changes you make. This is what the application does; a calendar that could not write would not be usable.
  • openid, email, profile — to show which account is connected, so that you can tell two connected accounts apart.

Google data is used solely to provide the calendar features you see in the app. It is never transferred to anyone, never used for advertising, never sold, and never used to train any model. No human, including the developer, has access to it.

Removing your data

  • Removing an account in the app deletes its stored credentials from your computer. You choose whether its calendars stay in xrncal as calendars on this computer or are deleted with it; neither changes anything at the provider.
  • To withdraw the app's access at Google independently of the app, visit myaccount.google.com/permissions and remove xrncal. Removing the account inside the app deletes the credential locally but does not by itself revoke the grant on Google's side.
  • Uninstalling the app and deleting its profile directory removes everything the app has stored, its automatic backups included; a backup you saved elsewhere yourself stays where you put it. Because the developer holds no copy, there is nothing left to request the deletion of.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and collects no information from anyone, including children.
